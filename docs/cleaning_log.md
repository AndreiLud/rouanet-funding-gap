# Cleaning log

Every decision here was taken after looking at what the data actually contains, and each
one names the number it was taken against. The counts come from
`reports/quality_report.md`, which is regenerated by `make quality`. Collection date:
2026-09-20.

## Collection integrity: no loss, no duplication

The API paginates by `offset` over a live table, so a sweep can duplicate or miss rows
if the table changes underneath it. It did not happen here. 618 pages parsed into 61,337
rows, which is exactly the total the API reported, with 61,337 distinct PRONACs, no row
sharing a PRONAC with another, and no PRONAC appearing under more than one area code.
Per area, the distinct PRONACs collected match the total the API reported for that area
before its sweep started.

**Decision**: accept the pull as complete. No deduplication step is needed, and one is
deliberately not added, because a silent `drop_duplicates` would have hidden exactly the
failure this check exists to catch.

## One corrupt raw page, refetched

`area=1/offset=0000700.json.gz` had a valid gzip header followed by garbage after the
first member. Cause: two collector processes ran at once by accident and interleaved
writes into the same scratch file. One page of 618.

**Decision**: delete and refetch that page, then harden the collector rather than work
around it. The scratch file name now carries the process id, every page is read back
before it counts as written, and the resume check reads a page rather than testing that
the file exists. All 618 pages now parse.

## Missing values

Only two columns have any: `data_inicio` (323, 0.5%) and `data_termino` (328, 0.5%).

**Decision**: keep the rows and carry the missingness explicitly. Project duration
becomes null for them rather than being imputed, and the model handles it as a missing
value. Imputing a duration from the cohort mean would invent a fact about the project.

## Negative money

36 rows have `valor_aprovado` below zero and 33 have `valor_projeto` below zero. A
negative approved amount is not a real quantity.

**Decision**: set these to null and drop the rows from the analysable sample, since the
target cannot be computed without an approved amount. Logged rather than clipped to
zero, because zero would silently become "approved for nothing" and enter the counts.

## Raised more than approved

1,006 approved projects (1.7%) have `valor_captado` above `valor_aprovado`. The median
ratio among them is 1.00, so most are at or just above the line and are consistent with
rounding or a small residual authorisation. 33 are above twice the approved amount, and
the maximum ratio is roughly 3.4e8, which is not a real fundraising outcome.

**Decision**: for the binary target this is irrelevant, since any ratio above 1 clears
every threshold. Keep the rows. For any continuous reporting of the share raised, the
ratio is capped at 1 and the cap is stated in the figure. The extreme rows are kept in
the sample and flagged, not deleted, because deleting them would quietly remove real
projects on the basis of one bad field.

## Approved amount of zero

575 rows have `valor_aprovado` equal to zero, and 18 of those have `valor_captado` above
zero, which is contradictory.

**Decision**: exclude all 575 from the analysable sample. The question is "what share of
the approved amount gets raised", which is undefined at zero. The 18 contradictory rows
are recorded here and not repaired.

## Categories

27 distinct UF values and all 27 are real federal units, so no UF cleaning is needed.
123 segmentos, 113 tipologias, 18 tipicidades, 8 areas, 2 mecanismos, 3 enquadramentos.

**Decision**: no category merging at this stage. Rare-level grouping is a modelling
choice, so it happens inside the pipeline where it is fitted on training folds only,
not here where it would leak across the split.

## The fundraising window, and which cohorts are usable

`situacao` has 66 distinct values, mapped to four states in `src/cohorts.py`:
`pre_approval`, `open`, `closed` and `withdrawn`. Every one of the 66 is matched by a
rule; none falls through.

The mapping was checked against the data rather than against the wording alone. Among
approved projects: `open` is 78.8% at zero raised, `withdrawn` is 91.3% at zero, and
`closed` is 61.5% at zero with a real spread above it. That is the pattern the labels
predict, so the mapping is doing what it claims.

**The finding that constrains the whole project**: cohorts 2019 to 2022 are 0.1% to 1.1%
open, and cohorts 2023 onwards are 22% to 90% open. Reading a rate off a partially open
cohort understates it badly, because the projects that close first are
disproportionately the ones closed for raising nothing. The apparent collapse from 44.2%
in 2022 to 8.5% in 2025 is that artefact, not a trend.

**Decision**: the analysable sample is cohorts 2019 to 2022 with a closed window and an
approved amount above zero. That is 12,722 projects. Cohorts 2023 onwards are described
in the exploratory analysis and excluded from modelling, with the reason stated.

## Approved projects sitting in an evaluation state

309 projects in the 2019 to 2022 cohorts have an approved amount above zero but a
`situacao` that reads as an evaluation step (`Edital - Encaminhado a Comissao de
Avaliacao`, `Analise de resposta de diligencia`, and similar). Their fundraising window
has long passed, so the status appears to be stale rather than current.

**Decision**: exclude them, which is 2.4% of the otherwise analysable 13,031. This is not
neutral and the direction is known: those 309 reach 50% of the approved amount at 55.7%,
against 39.9% in the sample that is kept, so excluding them lowers the headline rate
slightly. Recorded here rather than absorbed, and worth revisiting if the headline turns
out to be sensitive to it.

## Personal data

`cgccpf` and the proponent name never leave `data/raw`, which is untracked. The proponent
survives downstream only as a salted hash, and the salt is generated per checkout and is
not committed. Some proponents are individuals trading under a CNPJ whose registered name
contains their own name, so the name is dropped outright rather than filtered by person
type.

**Correction, made after the proponents endpoint finished collecting.** The API masks a
natural person's document, returning values like `***000*****`, and returns a company's
CNPJ in full. An earlier version of the parser inferred a natural person by testing for
an 11 digit CPF, which never matched, because stripping the mask characters leaves only
the few visible digits. The flag was therefore silently always False. It now tests for
the mask, which flags 11,672 of 61,337 projects, and the rule was checked against the
`tipo_pessoa` field on the proponents endpoint, where masking and the declared type agree
exactly.

Two consequences follow, and neither was visible before the correction:

- The raw pages contain complete CNPJs and masked CPFs, not complete CPFs. That is a
  weaker privacy exposure than the earlier note in this log implied, and the handling
  does not change: the document and the name still never leave `data/raw`.
- The hash identifying a natural person is a hash of a mask, so two individuals whose
  visible digits coincide become one proponent. 9,113 distinct masks cover 11,672
  projects and the largest covers 11, so the merging is small, and it can only overstate
  concentration. The README reports the bound from organisations only, where the document
  is complete and no merging is possible.
